Balancing Electricity Production, Water Supply and Reservoir Security

Multi-objective operating strategy analysis for the Yagisawa, Shimokubo and Kusaki reservoirs in the Upper Tone River basin, Japan. A simulation-based decision framework reveals how hydropower generation, dependable water delivery, flood-space requirements and year-end storage reserves compete under alternative operating rules.

Executive Summary

Quantifying operating compromises across a three-reservoir system

The analysis compares two families of reservoir operating policies against common performance measures: annual electricity generated, the dependability of meeting monthly water-supply obligations, unmet supply volume and the drawdown of water carried into the next operating period. Rather than equating maximum generation with the best strategy, the framework separates production leadership from the cost of reduced water reserves and exposes qualifying, non-dominated operating alternatives.

01 · System Context

A reservoir portfolio serving interconnected water and energy objectives

Yagisawa, Shimokubo and Kusaki are treated as a coordinated analytical portfolio in which storage, seasonal inflows, hydropower discharge and planned water deliveries define a shared decision challenge. Allocating space for flood preparedness changes available operational flexibility; releasing water for generation can diminish future supply protection.

Physical system

Monthly storage, inflows, releases, spill, operational losses and turbine discharge constraints form the reservoir continuity model.

Service system

Water-supply commitments are assessed as monthly performance obligations over simulated operating conditions.

Planning system

Flood-storage requirements and reserve protection shape the feasible range of energy-focused operating rules.

02 · Challenge

How can additional hydropower be evaluated without obscuring water-security exposure?

A strategy may generate more annual electricity and still impose a material cost through deeper year-end drawdown. Conversely, reserve-protective policies may sacrifice generation while maintaining subsequent-period operational flexibility. The challenge is to compare both outcomes under a common modeled physical system and make the trade-off legible to decision-makers.

Core decision question: Which qualifying operating strategy offers the most appropriate balance of generation potential, dependable water supply and carry-over water storage for the chosen planning mandate?
03 · Quantitative Framework

A shared physical accounting system with competing operating objectives

Reservoir continuity

For each month, closing storage equals opening storage plus inflow volume less controlled release, spill and modeled losses.

Hydropower conversion

Generated energy depends on turbine discharge, hydraulic head, conversion efficiency and the length of the operating period.

Operating objective

Policies balance annual electricity with explicit penalties on undelivered water and terminal storage deficit.

The common performance ledger evaluates achieved energy, water-service reliability, unmet supply and year-end storage. Flood reserve and supply commitment are planning inputs; they are not presented as measured flood benefits or realized economic returns.

07 · Scenario Analysis

Operating requirements, uncertainty interpretations and strategic priorities

49 operating requirement pairs

Seven flood-space targets crossed with seven water-supply targets establish a structured planning matrix.

98 operating policy evaluations

Two candidate methods are compared at each FC–WS pair under the same scalarized evaluation framework.

24 multi-objective alternatives

Six penalty combinations for each method under two interpretations of uncertain seasonal inflow dispersion.

The two uncertainty interpretations are sensitivity cases arising from ambiguous source dispersion labels, not validated independent forecasts. The analysis uses a theoretical simulation system and approximate optimizers.

08 · Results

Higher generation is visible—but so is the cost of lower closing reserves

333.4 versus 306.8 GWh

The dynamic-programming and genetic-search groups differ substantially in modeled annual production across identical planning target pairs.

120.5 versus 90.6 million m³

Mean terminal deficits are higher for the dynamic-programming portfolio, underscoring the importance of carry-over water valuation.

97 of 98 policies qualify

Most candidate policies meet the 90% modeled annual supply-dependability screen; this represents simulated frequency, not guaranteed service.

Evidence boundary: These results show modeled strategy compromises, not measured reservoir performance, financial return, guaranteed flood mitigation or globally optimal policy. The different optimizers are approximate candidate searches with distinct formulations.
09 · Decision Relevance

What the analysis enables for infrastructure strategy

Transparent operating mandates

Make the relative importance of generation, water-service commitments and reserve protection explicit instead of hiding it inside a single ranking.

Early policy screening

Eliminate candidate policies that fail a chosen modeled service standard before comparing energy-production opportunities.

Water-security stress awareness

Expose year-end drawdown consequences that may be overlooked when performance is viewed through electricity output alone.
