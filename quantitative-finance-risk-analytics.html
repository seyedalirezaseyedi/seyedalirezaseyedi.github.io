Interpretation

Lead–lag analysis examines whether movements in one company tend to be followed by movements in another. A strongest relationship near lag zero suggests largely contemporaneous co-movement; a persistent non-zero lag may indicate delayed information transmission or market-response differences. These relationships are descriptive and should be tested for stability before being used operationally.

Interpretation

The diagnostics assess how closely observed returns align with assumptions commonly used in Black–Scholes-style modelling. Large changes in volatility across horizons, significant non-normality, pronounced skewness or excess kurtosis, and serial dependence all signal greater model risk and can affect the reliability of standard option-pricing assumptions.

Interpretation

Heavy-tail diagnostics evaluate whether observed returns depart materially from a Gaussian distribution. Significant skewness, excess kurtosis and a low Jarque–Bera p-value indicate asymmetric or fat-tailed behaviour, conditions under which Gaussian models may understate extreme-move risk. The Lévy/NIG comparison illustrates how a richer return process can capture these features more flexibly.
