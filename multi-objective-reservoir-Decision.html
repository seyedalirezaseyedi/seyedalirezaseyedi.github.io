Stochastic Multi-Reservoir Operation & Strategy Optimization

An integrated decision-support framework for comparing operating strategies across the Yagisawa, Shimokubo and Kusaki reservoirs in Japan's upper Tone River basin. The analysis examines how water-supply commitments and flood-storage requirements influence annual hydropower, dependable service and water retained for future operations.

Explore modeled outcomes across 49 combinations of water-supply and flood-control requirements, compare 98 evaluated operating strategies, and assess 24 additional multi-objective alternatives. Performance measures come from a consistent simulated reservoir system and are presented as planning estimates—not measured electricity production, verified economic returns or live operating instructions.

Decision context

Electricity

Energy output measures physical production potential. A bankable revenue forecast additionally requires contracted prices, dispatch profiles, pumping consumption, and asset-level operating costs.

Supply service

Service dependability measures the proportion of assessed annual water conditions in which every monthly commitment is satisfied. It should inform service standards—not be interpreted as a contractual guarantee.

Water retained

Carryover storage protects flexibility entering the next planning cycle. A smaller year-end shortfall indicates lower reliance on drawing down strategic water reserves.

Complete Operating Results & Model Scope

Interpretation & confidence of decisions

This is a simulated-world planning instrument, not a forecast of actual production and not an approved control system. Its strengths are transparent trade-off calculations, common evaluation conditions, and numerical mass balance.

A broad range of possible annual inflow conditions

The hydrological model explores a broad range of potential 12-month inflow sequences using the available seasonal statistics. These scenarios represent plausible conditions under model assumptions; they are not recorded historical operating years.

Independent performance assessment across 800 annual scenarios

After producing a candidate operating rule, the assessment evaluates it against 800 separate annual operating scenarios outside policy development. This checks robustness within the assumed simulation model.

Reliability is a simulated frequency

For example, 99% means about 99 of every 100 simulated trial years meet all modeled monthly commitments. Uncertainty from model choice still applies.

Operating economics and calculation principles

Monthly water balance and losses
Ending storage = starting storage + 0.0864 × days × (inflow − release − spill) − losses Units: storage in million m³; flow in m³/s. Losses: assumed 0.1% of starting monthly storage, bounded by available water.
Monthly electricity generation
Energy (GWh) = [1000 × 9.81 × turbine efficiency × hydraulic head × turbine flow × 24 × days] / 1,000,000,000 Turbine flow cannot exceed the modeled turbine limit. Hydraulic head is approximated from simulated storage. Yagisawa pumped-storage pumping costs and cycles are not modeled.
Why operating strategies differ
Maximize: expected annual electricity − shortage penalty × annual undelivered water − storage penalty × year-end storage deficit The optimizer uses a GA feedback policy and approximate decomposed dynamic programming. The trade-off search varies the penalties, evaluates the outcomes and identifies candidate policies not dominated by another tested qualifying candidate.
Decision assurance and scope

Water balance and programmed storage constraints pass within floating-point tolerance in the provided computations. Maximum absolute residual ≈ 8.53×10⁻¹⁴ million m³. The optimization is approximate; inputs include reconstructed hydrology and assumed engineering parameters. No claim of real-world predictive validation, global optimality, certified flood safety, or economic benefit is made.
