01 · Market Context

Financial models depend on assumptions that can fail when market structure changes

Correlation, volatility, distribution shape, serial dependence and tail behaviour are not fixed properties. A useful quantitative-finance workflow therefore needs to examine whether assumptions remain plausible across the selected market window before relying on pricing or risk outputs.

Dependence dimension

Assets may move together contemporaneously or exhibit delayed relationships that vary with the selected period.

Model-risk dimension

Gaussian return assumptions, stable volatility and diffusion-based pricing can become less reliable when skewness, excess kurtosis or regime changes are material.

02 · Challenge

How can market dependence, model assumptions and pricing uncertainty be assessed in one coherent workflow?

The analytical challenge was to connect market-structure analysis with model diagnostics and derivative valuation, rather than treating correlation, volatility, option pricing and heavy-tail risk as unrelated exercises.

Core analytical challenge: Build a transparent workflow that reveals co-movement, delayed dependence, volatility shifts, distribution departures and pricing sensitivity before interpreting model outputs.

07 · Module Architecture

Seven analytical modules with distinct quantitative questions

01 · Time-Series ClusteringCompare normalized paths and aligned return correlations to identify market co-movement and similarity around a reference company.
02 · Lead–Lag EffectMeasure delayed return relationships, with the dashboard using a ±10-day lag range for selected companies.
03 · Black–Scholes TestAssess volatility stability, normality, skewness, kurtosis and serial dependence across selected financial companies.
04 · Change-Point DetectionIdentify the strongest single variance-regime change and compare pre- versus post-break volatility.
05 · Option PricingEstimate empirical volatility and compare at-the-money European-call values from Black–Scholes and Monte Carlo methods.
06 · Monte Carlo MethodExamine convergence, simulation uncertainty and terminal payoff behaviour using empirical calibration.
07 · Lévy / Heavy TailsEvaluate skewness, excess kurtosis and Gaussian-model adequacy, and retain source-reported Lévy/NIG outputs for comparison.
08 · Results & Diagnostics

Evidence-backed outputs focused on model interpretation rather than trading claims

Co-movement is descriptive, not causalCorrelation-based similarity identifies companies whose aligned daily return patterns move together more closely over the selected period.
Lead–lag relationships require stability checksA non-zero lag may indicate delayed response or information transmission, but the dashboard explicitly treats these relationships as descriptive.
Black–Scholes assumptions can be stress-testedVolatility shifts, significant non-normality, skewness, excess kurtosis and serial dependence all signal greater model risk.
Regime breaks expose changing risk conditionsChange-point analysis separates the return series into two candidate volatility regimes and compares pre/post annualized volatility.
Pricing methods can be cross-checked numericallyBlack–Scholes provides a closed-form benchmark while Monte Carlo estimates the same contract through simulated risk-neutral paths under aligned assumptions.
Heavy-tail diagnostics challenge Gaussian adequacySkewness, excess kurtosis and Jarque–Bera evidence help identify conditions where Gaussian models may understate extreme-move risk.
Source-specific Lévy evidence: the repository records an AXP Lévy call payoff of approximately 0.06857888 and discounted value of approximately 0.06857733 for the documented source setup. These are preserved as source-reported outputs, not generalized market values.
09 · Risk Relevance

Why This Matters for Quantitative Finance & Model Risk

The transferable value is the ability to test whether market data support the assumptions behind a quantitative model, compare alternative valuation approaches, and make simulation and distributional uncertainty visible before relying on a numerical result.

Market-structure diagnostics

Measure contemporaneous and delayed dependence across companies and selected market windows.

Model-risk assessment

Evaluate whether volatility stability and Gaussian return assumptions are plausible enough for standard diffusion-based modelling.

Derivative valuation consistency

Cross-check closed-form and Monte Carlo prices under aligned assumptions and inspect sensitivity to volatility.

Tail-risk awareness

Recognize when skewness and excess kurtosis suggest that richer stochastic processes may be needed for risk interpretation.
