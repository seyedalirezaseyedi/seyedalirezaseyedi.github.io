01 · System Context

Administrative structures and hydrological systems do not necessarily align

The Urmia Lake Basin provides a setting in which water demand, supply, administrative responsibilities and environmental constraints interact across multiple zones and sectors. The analytical problem is structural: a governance system may be administratively coherent while still being poorly aligned with the basin’s hydrological organization.

Institutional layer

Administrative arrangements determine how interactions are coordinated across governance cores and water-use sectors.

Hydrological layer

The basin’s environmental and water-system structure creates interdependencies that may not match administrative boundaries or responsibilities.

02 · Challenge

How can institutional–hydrological mismatch be quantified rather than described qualitatively?

The central challenge was to move from a descriptive governance assessment to a quantitative comparison of alternative structures, while retaining the interactions among basin zones, sectors and administrative configurations.

Core analytical challenge: How can institutional structures and hydrological systems be quantitatively compared to identify governance mismatch and evaluate alternative structural configurations?
03 · Quantitative Framework

A statistical-mechanics representation of governance fit

Curie–Weiss mean-field formulationRepresents system interactions in a statistical-mechanics framework adapted to water governance.
Hamiltonian structural system costProvides a quantitative measure of the structural cost associated with a given governance configuration.
Zonal pressure / gainSupports interpretation of where pressure or advantage is concentrated across the system under alternative configurations.
The dashboard extends the published framework into a scenario-analysis environment while preserving the underlying model logic and reproducing the workbook baseline to numerical precision.

09 · Decision Relevance

Why This Matters for Water & Environmental Decision Analytics

The transferable value is the ability to represent a complex institutional-environmental system quantitatively, compare alternative organizational structures on a common basis, and expose where governance design may be poorly matched to the physical system it is intended to manage.

Structural scenario comparison

Environmental-system alignment

Decision communication

Turn an abstract statistical-mechanics model into an interactive decision-support view for interpreting fit, cost and pressure.
